Why this 10 minutes matters

You're going to use AI tools in this course, in other classes, and in whatever comes after graduation, whether that's grad school, a lab, or a job. The question isn't if you'll use AI. It's whether you'll use it well.

Used carelessly, AI can hand you confident-sounding nonsense, quietly replace your own thinking, or get you in academic integrity trouble you didn't see coming.
Used well, it can help you study faster, test your understanding, and get unstuck on tough problems, without doing the thinking for you.

This module is short on purpose. Five quick stops: why it matters, what you already know, three core skills, a chance to practice, and a two-minute reflection.

Three skills, ninety seconds each

Skill 1

AI is confident even when it's wrong

Language models predict plausible-sounding text. They don't check facts against a database, they generate what statistically sounds right. That means they can invent citations, misstate dates, or fabricate quotes, all delivered in the same confident tone as correct information.

  • Treat AI output as a first draft, not a final answer.
  • Verify anything you'll actually rely on: dates, statistics, citations, formulas.
  • If it sounds oddly specific and you can't find it anywhere else, be suspicious.
Skill 2

Vague prompts get vague answers

"Help me with my essay" gets you generic filler. Good prompts give the model context, a clear task, and constraints.

Weak: "Explain photosynthesis"
Better: "Explain photosynthesis to a first-year biology student in 3 sentences, then give me two quiz questions to test myself."

Context + task + format = an answer you can actually use.

Skill 3

Disclosure isn't optional, it's expected

Every instructor has a different policy on AI use, from "not allowed" to "use it and cite it" to "use it freely." None of that matters if you don't check first.

  • Read the syllabus AI policy for each course, don't assume it's the same everywhere.
  • When in doubt, ask your instructor directly. It's a normal question, not a suspicious one.
  • If you use AI and it's allowed, say so. A one-line note ("Used Claude to brainstorm an outline") takes ten seconds and protects you.
